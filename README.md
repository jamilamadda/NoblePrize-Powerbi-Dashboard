🏅 Nobel Prize Analysis (1901–2016) | Power BI Dashboard
📌 Project Overview

This project explores historical Nobel Prize trends from 1901 to 2016 using an interactive Power BI dashboard. The objective was to analyze long-term patterns in demographics, geography, and institutional affiliation, and to present insights through clear, structured visual storytelling.

The project was completed as part of Week 7 of the Data Analytics program at Nashville Software School, with a focus on building multi-page analytical dashboards suitable for stakeholder exploration.

🎯 Objectives

Analyze Nobel Prize distribution across time, categories, and demographics

Identify patterns related to age, gender, and country of origin

Examine institutional and geographic concentration of award recipients

Practice structuring insights across multiple analytical layers

Build a recruiter-ready Power BI dashboard focused on clarity and usability

📊 Dashboard Structure & Insights
🔹 Page 1: High-Level Overview

This page provides a macro-level summary of the dataset:

969 Nobel Prizes awarded across all categories during the period

Interactive filters for:

Category

Gender

Laureate home country

Custom year range

A detailed laureate table that enables granular exploration while maintaining overall context

This page establishes a foundation for deeper trend analysis in subsequent views.

🔹 Page 2: Demographics & Distribution

This page focuses on who wins and when:

Age distribution analysis shows most laureates receive their prize in their 50s and 60s, with a peak around age 60

Gender breakdown highlights a significant gender imbalance, particularly within scientific categories

Shared prize analysis shows how often awards are split (up to a maximum of three recipients)

A Top 10 countries comparison reveals the United States leading with 276 laureates, significantly ahead of other nations

🔹 Page 3: Institutions, Geography & Equity

This page explores where excellence is concentrated:

Although Nobel Prizes are not awarded directly to universities, institutional affiliations reveal dominance by U.S. and European institutions

Notable institutions include Cambridge, Oxford, and Zurich

Gender disparities persist across institutions and categories, though recent years show gradual improvement

Geographic analysis indicates that approximately 90% of prizes are associated with Europe and the U.S., reflecting research infrastructure concentration and global academic migration

🔑 Key Findings

Nobel recognition is heavily concentrated by age, geography, and gender

Strong research ecosystems and institutional access play a critical role in award outcomes

While inequities remain, long-term trends show slow but measurable progress
